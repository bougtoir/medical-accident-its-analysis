# Candidate journals after IJHPM desk reject

Manuscript: *Regional variation in anaesthesia practice in Japan: structural determinant or claims-audit artefact?*

Date: 2026-07-23

## Summary

After a desk reject from the *International Journal of Health Policy and Management* (IJHPM), the next-best target is a journal with:

- an impact factor (IF) of at least 5,
- a hybrid publishing model so subscription publication is possible,
- and a scope that accommodates health services / claims-data research on anaesthesia practice variation.

The leading candidate is **Journal of Clinical Anesthesia**.

## Recommendation

### Primary target: Journal of Clinical Anesthesia

- **Why**: directly relevant to anaesthesiologists and perioperative clinicians; IF ≥ 5; hybrid model avoids APC if subscription is chosen; our clinical-claims / regional-variation / audit-artefact question fits its health-services and clinical-epidemiology scope.
- **Risk**: 5,000-word main-text limit will require tightening the Discussion and possibly moving the sensitivity-analysis details to supplementary material.

### Secondary targets

1. **Social Science & Medicine** — if we can strengthen the theoretical/policy framing.
2. **BMJ Quality & Safety** — if we can articulate clear quality-improvement and patient-safety implications.

### Non-recommended but worth noting

- **BMC Health Services Research**: best *conceptual* fit, but fully OA and IF < 5.
- **Health Policy and Planning**: good health-policy fit, fully OA, APC modest.
- **Health Policy**: governance/regulation fit, but IF < 5.

## High-JIF "twist" options

Even when the scope fit is good, a small reframing can target a higher-impact journal.

| Twist | Reframed hook | Candidate journal | IF | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Quality & safety / health equity** | "Geographic variation in anaesthesia technique as a marker of perioperative care equity under universal coverage" | BMJ Quality & Safety | 6.7–6.8 | Needs stronger patient-safety/quality-improvement implications and a clear equity message. |
| **Top-tier clinical anaesthesia** | "Structural determinants of anaesthesia practice variation: implications for workforce and patient outcomes" | British Journal of Anaesthesia | 9.2 | High bar; usually clinical or translational. Would need to connect variation to outcomes (e.g., CPSP, opioid use) in the Discussion, not just claims counts. |
| **Top-tier clinical anaesthesia (US)** | Same as above, with emphasis on health-services / perioperative population science | Anesthesiology | 9.4 | ASA journal; accepts population-health studies but competition is fierce. |
| **Broad health services / open data** | "A claims-data natural experiment on the limits of audit as a policy lever" | JAMA Network Open | ~5.4 | Fully OA, high visibility, broad scope; needs wider US/global relevance and clear policy implication. |
| **Environmental / sustainability angle** | "Regional anaesthesia vs general anaesthesia: a health-system view of resource and environmental impact" | Journal of Clinical Anesthesia / BJA | 5–9 | Requires additional data or assumptions; probably too speculative. |

### Realistic "one-twist" recommendation

If we keep the current data and only reframe the narrative, **BMJ Quality & Safety** is the most realistic higher-JIF target. The twist is to present the findings as:

1. **A quality-of-care / equity signal** — under uniform coverage, where you live determines access to neuraxial techniques.
2. **A surveillance use-case** — claims data + small-area analysis can monitor perioperative care variation.
3. **A policy lesson** — tightening audits will not fix variation; workforce/organisation interventions will.

This repositions the paper from "anaesthesia epidemiology" to "health system quality and safety" without new analyses.
