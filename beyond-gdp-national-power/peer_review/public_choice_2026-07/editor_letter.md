Dear Onishi,

Thank you for submitting "Network Exclusion and State Collapse: From Maritime Isolation to Technological Access Denial in the Long Run of History" to Public Choice. I have now received two referee reports and have read the manuscript carefully. The referees find the research question interesting and the project ambitious, but each recommends rejection. I agree with their recommendation. Regrettably, we cannot move forward with the current manuscript.

The primary concern involves the classification of technologically excluded polities on which the paper's main result depends. Both referees question whether the criteria and historical basis for those classifications are developed clearly enough to support the analysis, and one identifies several cases as historically contestable. That referee also does not regard the increased statistical significance of the closure-conquest association following reclassification as evidence that the classifications are valid. Because all seven reclassified polities were conquered, moving those cases from the open group to the excluded group necessarily strengthens the reported association.

The reports also question whether the manuscript demonstrates the mechanism connecting technological exclusion to state collapse. The proposed mechanism is plausible, but the paper would need to show that the relevant polities experienced a disruption in technological transmission, that this disruption weakened economic performance or state capacity, and that the resulting weakness increased their vulnerability to conquest. The current analysis largely assumes this sequence rather than establishing it.

Addressing these concerns would require rebuilding the classification framework and developing new evidence for the proposed mechanism rather than revising the current analysis. Both referees and I nevertheless find the topic interesting and see the potential for a contribution to Public Choice. I would thus be willing to consider a new paper on the topic that handles the issues the referees raise, but it would need to be treated as a new submission.

Regardless of how you decide to proceed, I hope you find the referees' reports useful. Thank you for the opportunity to read your manuscript.

Sincerely,

Colin Harris
Associate Editor
