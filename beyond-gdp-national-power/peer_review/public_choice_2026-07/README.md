## Key concerns raised

### Reviewer 1

1. **Introduction / framing**: The jump to the stock/flow classification on page 3 is abrupt; the relevance of that classification for the research question is not explained.
2. **Missing literature review**: Key Public Choice literature (Piano 2019; Boettke & Candela 2020; Hendrickson, Salter & Albrecht 2018; Geloso & Salter 2020; Piano & Salter 2021) and relevant empirical work (Bologna Pavlik & Young 2019; Olsson & Hibbs 2005; Flückiger et al. 2022; Fernández-Villaverde et al. 2023; Young 2016; Bastos 2025; Rodríguez & Imam 2025) are absent.
3. **Classification and data construction**: No explanation of how classifications were made, which sources were consulted, or how the geographic-barrier index was constructed. No summary statistics or maps of the 96 polity-periods.
4. **Methods**: Methods are listed but identification assumptions are not justified. The 2SLS IV (geographic barrier → closure) lacks a first-stage regression and is likely underpowered. PSM lacks covariate-balance tests; coarse exact matching may be more appropriate for coarse/discrete covariates.
5. **Inference and standard errors**: Randomization inference is praised, but spatial correlation is flagged as a major concern; Conley & Kelly (2025) suggested. Bastos (2025, Public Choice) provides a relevant template using effective regression weights, omitted-variable-bias tests, and Conley standard errors.

### Reviewer 2

1. **Classification of technologically excluded polities**: Seven reclassified polities are historically contestable (Timurid Empire landlocked? Han Empire and Silk Road? Kievan Rus and Dnieper route?). The classification appears internally contradictory and potentially post hoc.
2. **Concerns applying criteria**: All seven reclassified polities were conquered, so moving them from "open" to "excluded" mechanically strengthens the closure-conquest association. The author needs a blind, pre-specified coding strategy.
3. **Mechanism gaps**: No evidence that technological exclusion disrupted transmission, reduced economic performance, or weakened state capacity.
4. **Political economy story**: Selectorate theory is introduced but not connected to the empirical narrative; the Public Choice angle is underdeveloped.

## Editor's conclusion

> "Addressing these concerns would require rebuilding the classification framework and developing new evidence for the proposed mechanism rather than revising the current analysis. ... I would thus be willing to consider a new paper on the topic that handles the issues the referees raise, but it would need to be treated as a new submission."

## Next steps for a new submission

1. **Classification rubric**: Develop an explicit, pre-specified, blind-applied coding rubric for network exclusion, with record-level sources. Distinguish structural exclusion from policy closure and from being on a non-dominant route.
2. **Data construction**: Document all coding decisions with primary/secondary sources; add summary statistics and maps; compare the geographic-barrier index and technology proxies to existing datasets (e.g., Comin et al. 2010, Roman road connectivity).
3. **Mechanism**: Show, for excluded polities, (a) disruption in technological transmission, (b) relative economic/state-capacity decline, and (c) increased vulnerability to conquest, relative to both open and policy-closed comparators.
4. **Methods**: Justify identification; report first-stage and balance diagnostics; consider coarse exact matching or randomization-inference with spatial correlation adjustments (Conley–Kelly / Bastos approach).
5. **Literature review**: Engage with the Public Choice and economic-history literature cited by Reviewer 1.
6. **Journal and format**: Because Public Choice treats this as a new submission, the next version should be a manuscript-level rewrite and a fresh submission (possibly to Public Choice or to a comparable journal).
