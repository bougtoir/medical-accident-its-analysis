## Key concerns raised

1. **Classification of technologically excluded polities**: The reviewers question the criteria and historical basis for the seven reclassified polities. The editor notes that moving all seven conquered cases from "open" to "excluded" necessarily strengthens the closure-conquest association, and that the increased statistical significance is therefore not evidence that the classification is valid.
2. **Mechanism**: The paper assumes, rather than demonstrates, that technological exclusion disrupted technological transmission, weakened economic performance/state capacity, and increased vulnerability to conquest.
3. **Political economy fit**: Selectorate theory is introduced but not tied to the empirical narrative, and Public Choice's political-economy angle is underdeveloped.

## Editor's conclusion

> "Addressing these concerns would require rebuilding the classification framework and developing new evidence for the proposed mechanism rather than revising the current analysis. ... I would thus be willing to consider a new paper on the topic that handles the issues the referees raise, but it would need to be treated as a new submission."

## Next steps

- A new paper would need (a) an explicit, pre-registered classification rubric with record-level sources, (b) independent coding/blind application of the rubric, (c) direct evidence of technological/economic/state-capacity deterioration in excluded polities, and (d) a clear causal mechanism tested against alternative explanations.
- Because Public Choice treats this as a new submission rather than a revise-and-resubmit, the next version should be a manuscript-level rewrite and a fresh submission.
