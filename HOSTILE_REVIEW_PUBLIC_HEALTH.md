# Hostile reviewer assessment — Public Health draft

Six hostile personas applied to the repositioned Public Health manuscript.
Each gives the most damaging accurate attack, whether it survives the
reframing, and what (if anything) was done.

## R1 — The epidemiologist who hates ecological designs

Attack: "Ecological specialty-level panel; you cannot infer anything about
individual physicians' location decisions, yet the whole motivation is about
career choices. Null findings from ecological data are especially
uninformative."

Status: partially survives. The abstract conclusion now says the analysis
"cannot speak to individual career decisions"; Discussion lists this as
limitation (1); the paper claims only a bounded specialty-level statement.
Residual risk: a reviewer may still ask why anyone should care — mitigated by
explicitly framing TOST as bounding aggregate, planning-relevant magnitudes.

## R2 — The health-services researcher who wants individual-level data

Attack: "Workload, remuneration, urbanisation and sex composition all drive
specialty choice. With no adjustment for any of these, an equivalence claim
about litigation is over-reaching."

Status: survives by scope narrowing. Methods now state the design "tests
whether litigation exposure alone could plausibly account for the magnitude of
specialty-level change", and limitations (5) state residual confounding is not
ruled out for other determinants. The claim is deliberately about litigation's
contribution, not a full causal model of supply.

## R3 — The medical-legal expert who says malpractice ≠ litigation

Attack: "Your exposure counts only closed civil claims from the Supreme Court
release. Criminal proceedings, out-of-court settlements, Model Compromise
Events, media coverage and reputational fear are absent or measured only in a
small matched subset (JMSR). Mismeasured exposure attenuates toward zero, so
your 'equivalence' may be an artefact."

Status: survives in wording. Exposure-measurement caveat added to limitations
(3): closed civil claims are an incomplete exposure signal; JMSR subset is
small; if mismeasurement dominates, bounds may understate a true effect.
Discussion now notes criminal prosecution had a documented effect (Morita) —
our exposure excludes it. This is the strongest remaining weakness and is now
stated rather than hidden.

## R4 — The health-policy scholar who sees malpractice fear as everywhere

Attack: "Physicians behave on perceived risk, not actual claim rates. A
coefficient on measured closed claims tells you nothing about deterrent
effects operating through perception and defensive practice."

Status: survives. Discussion explicitly separates perceptions/defensive
behaviour from measured supply outcomes (intro para 2 and defensive-medicine
paragraph), and concludes the deterrent story "is not supported at the
specialty level within the tested range" — not that perception effects are
absent.

## R5 — The methods purist who hates null equivalence claims

Attack: "±1%/±2% margins chosen by the authors are arbitrary and post-hoc;
you can always find a margin that gives 'equivalence'. Effect size per SD is
also sample-scale dependent."

Status: partially survives. Methods now say margins "were specified for the
present analysis, as changes small enough to be unlikely to alter national
specialty-workforce planning over a biennial interval" — honest rather than
falsely claiming preregistration. Limitations (2) state margins were chosen
by the authors and wider margins would weaken conclusions. Results report the
±1% hospital test as NOT equivalent, which demonstrates the analysis can fail
to confirm equivalence — a credibility point made explicitly in Discussion.

## R6 — The editor worried about originality ("null result, descriptive study")

Attack: "This is a null ecological finding dressed up as policy relevance.
Where is the contribution?"

Status: survives by repositioning. The contribution is now threefold and
stated plainly: (i) first specialty-level test of the litigation–supply link
in Japan using rate-scaled, un-interpolated data; (ii) bounded-equivalence
evidence separating "no evidence of effect" from "evidence against
policy-relevant effect"; (iii) correction of mechanical size-confounding that
inflated earlier count-based claims. International parallels (US, New Zealand,
Taiwan) situate the result for Public Health's readership.

## Summary

| Persona | Attack survives reframing? | Manuscript response |
|---------|----------------------------|---------------------|
| R1 Ecological critic | Partly | Scope claimed only at specialty level; limitation (1) |
| R2 Individual-data demander | No (scope narrowed) | Design statement + limitation (5) |
| R3 Exposure mismeasurement | Yes — honest caveat added | Limitations (3), Morita contrast |
| R4 Perception-driven deterrence | No | Perception ≠ measured supply distinction |
| R5 Margin arbitrariness | Partly | "specified for the present analysis"; limitation (2); ±1% hospital failure shown |
| R6 Originality editor | No | Three contributions stated in Discussion |

No attack invalidates the frozen analysis; all surviving attacks are now
acknowledged in-text rather than concealed.
